# Banking Customer & Financial Analysis

**SQL | Excel | Data Cleaning | Data Analysis | Business Reporting**

## Executive Summary

This project explores customer banking activity, account balances, transaction behaviour and lending performance to understand where the bank is performing well, where financial risk may exist, and where data could support better business decisions.

The analysis covers **1,500 customers, 2,100 accounts, 15,000 transactions, 900 loans and 6,000 loan payments**. I used SQL to analyse the underlying data, Excel to build pivot tables and a dashboard, and business reporting to translate the findings into practical recommendations.

The analysis identified strong customer deposit activity, growing reliance on digital banking channels, significant demand for personal lending, seasonal changes in loan demand, and a meaningful level of late or missed loan payments.

The overall finding is that the bank has clear opportunities for growth, but lending growth should be balanced with stronger repayment-risk monitoring.

---

## Business Problem

A bank needs to understand more than how much money customers hold or how many loans are issued.

The business also needs to understand:

- Where customer deposits are concentrated
- How customers are using different banking channels
- Which loan products generate the most demand
- When lending demand is strongest
- Which geographic markets show different financial behaviours
- How repayment behaviour may affect lending risk

The goal of this project was therefore to use customer, account, transaction and loan data to identify patterns that could help the bank improve customer engagement, allocate resources more effectively, support responsible lending and manage repayment risk.

---

## Business Questions

The analysis focused on the following questions:

1. Where are customer account balances concentrated?
2. Which account types hold the most customer deposits?
3. Which banking channels generate the most transaction activity?
4. Which loan products have the strongest demand?
5. How does loan demand change throughout the year?
6. How does lending activity differ across cities?
7. What does loan repayment behaviour indicate about potential credit risk?
8. Where could the bank improve efficiency, customer value and risk management?

---

## Methodology

### 1. Data Cleaning

Before analysing the data, I performed data-quality checks to prepare the dataset for analysis.

The cleaning process included:

- Checking the datasets for duplicate values
- Identifying and removing duplicate records
- Checking fields for consistency
- Reviewing the structure and relationships between tables
- Ensuring the data was suitable for SQL analysis and Excel reporting

This step was important because inaccurate or duplicated records could distort transaction totals, account balances, loan values and repayment results.

### 2. SQL Analysis

SQL was used to analyse customer, account, transaction, loan and loan-payment data.

The analysis focused on:

- Aggregations and totals
- Grouping and segmentation
- Transaction and lending trends
- Geographic comparisons
- Loan-type analysis
- Repayment-status analysis

### 3. Excel Analysis & Dashboard

Excel was then used to build supporting pivot tables, calculations and a static dashboard to present the results in a more accessible format.

The dashboard focused on:

- Account balances
- Transaction activity
- Loan demand
- Loan type
- Geographic patterns
- Loan repayment status

### 4. Business Reporting

The final stage was translating the analytical results into business implications and recommendations rather than simply reporting the numbers.

## Key Findings & Business Actions

### 1. Strong Customer Deposit Base

Customer accounts hold approximately **R62.3 million** in total balances.

Savings accounts represent the largest share at approximately **R31.1 million**, or around **50% of total account balances**.

**Business action:**

The bank has a strong opportunity to deepen relationships with customers who already hold significant deposits.

Higher-deposit markets could be targeted with:

- Investment products
- Premium banking services
- Wealth-building products
- Customer retention campaigns

This could help the bank increase customer value without relying entirely on acquiring new customers.

---

### 2. Digital Banking Is Becoming an Important Customer Channel

The **Mobile App** generated approximately **R6.6 million** in transaction value, making it the strongest transaction channel.

Online Banking followed at approximately **R4.7 million**, while POS, ATM and Branch transactions generated approximately R4.2 million, R3.3 million and R2.0 million respectively.

The Mobile App also recorded approximately **4,869 transactions**, compared with 3,384 through Online Banking.

**Business action:**

The bank should continue improving its digital banking experience and use these channels for more than routine transactions.

Digital channels could support:

- Self-service banking
- Personalised product recommendations
- Payment reminders
- Targeted savings and lending offers
- Lower-cost customer communication

The lower level of branch transaction activity also creates an opportunity to focus branch resources on higher-value services such as financial advice, lending and relationship management.

---

### 3. Personal Loans Represent the Largest Lending Segment

The bank processed **900 loan applications** with a combined value of approximately **R153.7 million**.

Personal loans represent the largest lending category at approximately **R51.3 million**, followed by:

- Home loans — approximately R34.0 million
- Vehicle loans — approximately R25.7 million
- Business loans — approximately R25.0 million
- Education loans — approximately R17.6 million

Personal lending therefore represents roughly one-third of total loan value.

**Business action:**

Personal loans represent an important growth opportunity, but high demand should not automatically be treated as a reason to increase lending.

The bank should compare:

**Loan demand → repayment performance → customer characteristics → risk**

This would help identify which lending segments offer attractive growth while maintaining acceptable levels of credit risk.

---

### 4. Loan Demand Changes Significantly Throughout the Year

Loan demand is not consistent throughout the year.

June recorded the highest monthly loan value at approximately **R21.5 million**, while September recorded the lowest at approximately **R8.4 million**.

March also showed strong demand at approximately **R17.3 million**.

**Business action:**

The bank can use these seasonal patterns to improve operational planning.

During stronger lending periods, the bank could prepare additional capacity for:

- Loan application processing
- Credit assessment
- Customer support
- Lending campaigns

During weaker periods, resources could instead support:

- Customer retention
- Refinancing opportunities
- Targeted acquisition campaigns
- Existing-customer engagement

This can help the bank plan resources around actual demand rather than treating lending activity as consistent throughout the year.

---

### 5. Repayment Behaviour Is the Main Risk Indicator

Approximately:

- **81.8%** of payments were made on time
- **13.6%** were late
- **4.7%** were missed

This means approximately **18.2% of payments were either late or missed**.

While most payments are made on time, the proportion that is not represents a meaningful credit-risk and collections concern.

**Business action:**

The bank should avoid focusing only on increasing loan volumes.

It should also strengthen early identification of customers who may be moving towards repayment difficulties.

Potential actions include:

- Early-warning indicators
- Automated payment reminders
- Personalised repayment communication
- Direct-debit or automated-payment options
- Stronger affordability assessments
- Monitoring repayment performance by loan type and customer segment

The bank should also investigate whether repayment problems are concentrated within particular loan types, cities, employment groups or income bands.

This would allow risk management to become more targeted rather than applying the same approach to every borrower.

---

### 6. Deposit and Lending Behaviour Differ Across Cities

Cape Town has the highest total account balance at approximately **R7.0 million**, followed by Pretoria and Mbombela at approximately **R6.6 million** each.

However, the cities with the highest loan demand are different.

Kimberley has the highest loan value at approximately **R18.4 million**, followed by Polokwane at approximately **R17.5 million** and East London at approximately **R17.4 million**.

**Business action:**

This suggests that the bank should avoid applying the same strategy across every geographic market.

For example:

- Higher-deposit markets could be targeted for savings, investment and premium products.
- Higher-loan-demand markets could receive more focused lending strategies.
- Lower-balance markets could be investigated further before being considered weaker markets.

This could help the bank allocate marketing and customer-engagement resources more effectively.

---

## Overall Business Priorities

Based on the analysis, the bank's main priorities should be:

### 1. Grow Customer Value

Use the existing deposit base to identify opportunities for investment, premium and wealth-building products.

### 2. Continue Digital Investment

Use the Mobile App and other digital channels to improve customer experience, reduce reliance on branches for routine activity and support lower-cost customer engagement.

### 3. Improve Lending Targeting

Focus lending growth on products and customer segments where demand is strong and repayment performance remains acceptable.

### 4. Strengthen Repayment Risk Monitoring

Use early-warning indicators and targeted communication to identify customers showing signs of repayment difficulty before problems become more serious.

### 5. Use Geographic and Seasonal Patterns

Use differences between cities and changes in monthly loan demand to improve marketing, staffing and operational planning.

Overall, the analysis supports **data-driven, risk-aware growth** rather than focusing only on increasing transaction or loan volumes.

---

## Limitations

This analysis is based on the available dataset and the static Excel dashboard and pivot-table outputs.

The dashboard does not provide interactive filtering, so the analysis focuses on overall patterns rather than allowing users to dynamically investigate individual customer segments.

The analysis also identifies relationships and patterns in the available data but does not establish causation.

For example, further analysis would be required to determine **why** certain cities, loan types or customer groups demonstrate different financial behaviours.

## Final Takeaway

This project demonstrated how banking data can be used to understand both **growth opportunities and financial risk**.

The bank has a strong deposit base, growing digital activity and significant lending demand. However, approximately **18.2% of loan payments are late or missed**, showing why lending growth needs to be balanced with stronger repayment monitoring.

The key lesson from the analysis is that better banking decisions come from looking at **customer behaviour, financial activity and risk together**.

By strengthening digital engagement, targeting lending more effectively, using geographic and seasonal patterns for planning, and identifying repayment difficulties earlier, the bank can make better use of its data while supporting more sustainable growth.
